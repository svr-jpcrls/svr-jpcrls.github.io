What a Cybersecurity Seminar Taught Me About Protecting What We Build

November 2025 John Paul F Carlos

On November 5, 2025, I attended IThrive Securely: Trends and Threats Behind Cybersecurity, a seminar held in person at Bulacan State University – Hagonoy Campus. I honestly didn't know what to expect going in, but I walked out thinking differently about my own habits and my responsibilities as a future IT professional.

What Was Discussed

Most of the seminar focused on practical security habits, particularly around password management. The speakers covered how to create strong passwords using unique characters, why you should never reuse the same password across different accounts, the value of using a password manager, and why updating your passwords regularly matters more than most people realize.

The part that hit hardest was the discussion on Multi-Factor Authentication. One of the speakers put it plainly, Two-Factor Authentication is basic hygiene now, because attackers can penetrate accounts far too easily. His point was simple, if you have credentials on any app, whether that's Facebook, Instagram, or Google, MFA should already be on. No exceptions.

What It Made Me Realize

It was eye-opening because we spend so much time in school learning how to build systems, but rarely talk about how to protect them. Most of my coursework up to that point had been about creating, writing code, designing dashboards, structuring databases. Security was always treated as someone else's concern.

That framing shifted after this seminar.

What I Learned

I went home and changed several of my passwords. More importantly, I started thinking about how data privacy connects directly to what I want to do as a data analyst. When we collect data, clean it, and visualize it, we are handling real information about real people. That comes with responsibility, not just technical skill.

Cybersecurity isn't only for security specialists. Anyone who touches data needs to understand the basics of protecting it.
