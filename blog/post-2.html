From Prompt to Production: Building Real AI Applications for Production

March 2026 John Paul F Carlos

On March 16, 2025, I attended "From Prompt to Production: Building Real AI Applications" at Bulacan State University – Hagonoy Campus. The guest speaker was Mr. Andrew Loyd Bulacan, a BulSU–Hagonoy alumnus who has since built a career working with AI and real-world applications. That detail alone made the whole seminar feel different, here was someone who sat in the same classrooms, and made it into a field that everyone is talking about right now.

What Was Discussed

The seminar covered how Large Language Models work, what's actually happening when you send a prompt and get a response. More practically, it introduced a structured approach to prompting such as Role, Task, Context, and Format. The idea is that a good prompt isn't just a question, it tells the AI who it should act as, what you need it to do, the context around the task, and how you want the answer delivered.

He also walked through how AI concepts can be turned into actual applications not just using existing tools, but thinking about how to build with AI as a foundation.

What Stood Out

One thing he said stuck with me is that artificial intelligence will not replace developers. It will empower those who are capable of bridging AI with real-world problems. That reframing matters. The concern most students have is whether AI will make their skills irrelevant. His answer was essentially only if you treat it as a threat instead of a tool.

He was also asked whether AI should be used as an academic tool. He said yes, that educational institutions can use AI to enhance learning, and that sooner or later, society will move into an AI-driven era regardless. Better to learn how to use it well now than to avoid it.

How It Changed My Thinking

Before this seminar, I was using AI casually asking quick questions and accepting whatever came back. After, I realized that AI is a skill, not just a shortcut. How you use it determines how much value you actually get from it. A vague prompt gives you a vague answer. A structured prompt gives you something you can actually work with.

It was motivating specifically because the speaker came from BulSU Hagonoy. It made the whole thing feel less abstract someone from the same campus figured this out, which means it's reachable.

What I Started Exploring After

After the seminar I started thinking about how AI connects to data analytics specifically. I began exploring how AI tools could help with tasks like summarizing datasets, generating Python code for analysis, or explaining what a function does. The Role+Task+Context+Format structure turned out to be genuinely useful, being specific about what I need and in what format makes the output much more practical.

AI won't do the thinking for you. But if you know how to direct it, it can make you significantly faster at the parts that used to slow you down.
