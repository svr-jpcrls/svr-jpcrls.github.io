What an Entrepreneurship Program Taught an IT Student

March 2026 John Paul F Carlos

During my time at Bulacan State University, I completed the Entrepreneurship Training Program under Ignite Philippines and the Wadhwani Foundation. It was structured like a course with lessons to go through, questions to answer, and activities to complete at each stage. Not the typical subject I expected to take as an IT student, but it turned out to be more relevant than I thought.

How It Was Structured

The program wasn't a seminar you just sit through. It had an actual flow, lessons on business concepts, followed by questions to check your understanding, then activities where you applied what you learned. Topics covered idea generation, building a business model, and basic financial planning. Each section built on the previous one, so by the end you had worked through a complete business concept from scratch.

What I Actually Did

The activities pushed you to think practically. Generating an idea sounds easy until you have to justify it who is it for, what problem does it solve, why would someone pay for it. The business model section made that thinking more structured, and the financial planning part forced you to put actual numbers behind the idea instead of keeping everything vague.

For someone studying IT, it was a different kind of problem-solving. Less about code and more about context, understanding why you're building something, not just how.

Why It Matters for an IT Student

Data analytics doesn't exist in isolation. Every dashboard, every report, every analysis is ultimately serving a business decision. Going through this program made me think more carefully about that side of the work, who is asking for the data, what decision they're trying to make, and what outcome they're working toward.

Knowing how to analyze data is one skill. Knowing how to frame it in terms of business value is another. This program gave me a small but useful foundation in the second one.
